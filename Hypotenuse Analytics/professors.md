**Prof. Sauvik Banerjee - IIT Bombay, Department of Civil Engineering**  
Research focus - Structural-health-monitoring (SHM) of concrete, steel and composite structures using ultrasonic guided-wave sensing, acoustic-emission, electromechanical-impedance and physics-informed machine-learning algorithms. He integrates AI for damage localisation, defect classification and long-range pipeline monitoring. Recent work includes a machine-learning-enabled ultrasonic guided-wave SHM system (2022-2025) and an indigenous corrosion-monitoring platform for reinforced-concrete (2022) [1][2].

**Prof. Ganesh Ramakrishnan - IIT Bombay, Department of Computer Science & Engineering**  
Research focus - AI-driven video-surveillance analytics (real-time intrusion, loitering, crowd-counting, face-recognition) under the “Surakshavyuh” platform, plus offline video-search “Jigyasa” and video-summarisation “Visiocity”. The system emphasises data-efficient deep learning for resource-constrained deployments. Projects - Surakshavyuh (real-time CCTV analytics), Jigyasa (text/face search), Visiocity (domain-specific video condensation) [3].

**Prof. Chetan Arora - IIT Delhi, Department of Computer Science & Engineering**  
Research focus - Computer-vision and trustworthy AI, including adversarial-attack/defence, reliability and fairness. Current work applies deep-learning models to medical-image cancer detection (breast, gall-bladder) and to camera-based monitoring tasks such as abnormal-event detection and multi-label classification for chest-X-ray and diabetic-retinopathy. Recent projects - Domain-generalised multi-label classifiers, multimodal foundational-model adaptation for mammography, and video-segment fakeness scoring [4].

**Prof. Vireshwar Kumar - IIT Delhi, Department of Computer Science & Engineering**  
Research focus - Security and privacy for cyber-physical systems, with AI-based intrusion-detection, adversarial-robust keyword spotting and side-channel-attack mitigation. His group designs detection mechanisms that counter AI-generated attacks on vehicular and IoT networks, a capability relevant to deep-fake-style sensor spoofing. Key publications - “SpotOn: Adversarially robust keyword spotting” (AsiaCCS 2024) and “ZBCAN: Zero-byte CAN defense” (USENIX 2023) .

**Prof. Sudipan Saha - IIT Delhi, Department of Computer Science & Engineering**  
Research focus - Deep-learning for remote-sensing and multi-temporal image change detection, including unsupervised domain-adaptation and multi-modal feature extraction. His work supports sensor-fusion pipelines for environmental monitoring and disaster assessment, aligning AI with large-scale imaging sensors. Recent papers - “Unsupervised change-detection using convolutional auto-encoders” (IGARSS 2019) and “Unsupervised multi-temporal change vector analysis” (IEEE GRS 2022) .

**Prof. Anirban Roy - IIT Kanpur, Department of Intelligent Systems**  
Research focus - Fundamental algorithms for multi-sensor data fusion in autonomous platforms, covering RF-based sensing, LiDAR, vision and inertial modalities. He develops lightweight AI models that fuse heterogeneous sensor streams for real-time perception in UAVs and smart-city applications. Recent activity - A two-part IEEE SPS talk (2025) on sensor-fusion concepts and applications [5][6].

**Prof. Dhiman Mallick - IIT Delhi, Department of Electrical Engineering**  
Research focus - MEMS/NEMS energy-harvesting, magnetic, magnetoelectric and piezoelectric transducers, and self-powered sensing platforms for autonomous IoT nodes. His group integrates AI-based signal processing to optimise harvest-and-sense cycles, enabling long-term deployment of environmental and biomedical sensors. Recent outputs - Patents on nonlinear electromagnetic harvesters (2023) and a review on hybrid transduction nanocomposite pressure sensors (Nano Energy 2023) .

**Prof. Pranjal Chandra - IIT (BHU) Varanasi, School of Biochemical Engineering**  
Research focus - Nanobiosensors and lab-on-a-chip platforms for point-of-care diagnostics, employing electrochemical, optical and AI-enhanced signal-analysis techniques. Projects span 3-D gold-dendrite immunosensors for carcino-embryonic antigen, metal-organic-framework based detection of antibiotics and AI-driven data analytics for rapid disease screening. Selected publications - “Ultrasensitive electrochemical biosensor for serotonin” (Biosensors & Bioelectronics 2020) and “Handbook of Nano-bioelectrochemistry” (Springer 2024) .

**Prof. Prafulla Kumar Behera - IIT Madras, Department of Physics**  
Research focus - Next-generation particle-detector sensors that improve medical-imaging resolution; the work merges high-precision detector physics with AI-assisted image reconstruction. An MoU with Israeli partners aims to fabricate the sensor for CERN experiments and translate the technology to clinical imaging. Recent news - Development of the sensor for particle detection and medical imaging, with fabrication planned in Israel and testing at CERN .

**Prof. R. V. Raghavendra Sai - IIT Madras, Department of Applied Mechanics & Biomedical Engineering**  
Research focus - Fiber-optic and nanophotonic biosensors for ultra-sensitive biomarker detection, integrating AI-based pattern-recognition to interpret optical spectra in real time. The lab targets cancer-diagnostic proteins (e.g., IGFBP2) and infectious-disease markers, coupling sensor hardware with machine-learning classifiers for point-of-care deployment. Recent activities - Development of a 3-D printed fiber-optic sensor for IGFBP2 detection and AI-driven data analytics pipeline [7].

**Prof. Ganesh Ramakrishnan - IIT Bombay, Department of Computer Science & Engineering** *(re-listed for emphasis on AI-camera surveillance)*  
Research focus - Real-time AI video analytics for security and public-health monitoring, including mask-wear detection, crowd-density alerts and autonomous anomaly detection in CCTV streams. The Surakshavyuh platform employs data-efficient deep-learning models that run on edge devices, enabling scalable surveillance without heavy cloud reliance. Key deliverables - Deployment at a naval dockyard, campus-wide COVID-era monitoring and open-source “DECILE” learning framework [3].
