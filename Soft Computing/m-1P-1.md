# What is Defuzzification?

I know:

**Defuzzification = Fuzzy output → Crisp output**

Why?
Because machines/actions need exact values.

Example:
Temperature is "High" (fuzzy) → Fan speed = **80 RPM** (crisp)

---

# Types of Defuzzification

1. Lambda-cut method
2. Maxima methods
3. Centroid methods
4. Weighted average method

---

# 1. Lambda-cut Method (α-cut)

My understanding:

I draw a horizontal line at λ and keep only values above it.

Formula:

Aλ = {x | μA(x) ≥ λ}

Meaning:
- Above λ → keep ✅
- Below λ → remove ❌

Example:

A = {(x1,0.9),(x2,0.5),(x3,0.2),(x4,0.3)}

For λ = 0.6:

A₀.₆ = {x1}

Why?
Only 0.9 ≥ 0.6

Memory trick:
**Lambda-cut = horizontal knife**

---

# Lambda-cut for relation

Same idea on matrix.

Example:

R =
0.2 0.3
0.9 0.5

For λ = 0.5

R₀.₅ =
0 0
1 1

Just thresholding.

# 2. Maxima Methods

My idea:

Only look at highest membership.

Step:
Find height first.

h(C) = max μ(x)

Example:

Hot Temp =
{(20,0),(25,0.2),(30,0.4),(35,0.6),(40,0.8),(45,1),(50,1)}

Maximum = 1
at 45 and 50

---

## Height Method

Use only if maximum is unique.

If one peak exists:
answer = that peak

Not usable here because two maxima.

---

## First of Maxima (FoM)

Take first maximum.

Answer = **45°C**

Formula:
x* = min{x | μ(x)=max}

---

## Last of Maxima (LoM)

Take last maximum.

Answer = **50°C**

Formula:
x* = max{x | μ(x)=max}

---

## Mean of Maxima (MoM)

Average all maxima.

(45 + 50)/2 = **47.5°C**

Formula:
x* = average of all max points

---

# Summary (Maxima)

Height → unique max  
FoM → first max  
LoM → last max  
MoM → average of maxima

---

# 3. Centroid Methods

My favorite:
Find balance point of fuzzy graph.

Like balancing cardboard on finger.

Most accurate.

---

## Center of Gravity (CoG)

Uses full graph.

Formula (continuous):

x* = ∫xμ(x)dx / ∫μ(x)dx

---

## Discrete CoG

Most common in numericals:

x* = Σ(xi μ(xi)) / Σμ(xi)

Example:

x:   10 20 30 40 50 60 70
μ: 0.2 .5 .8 .7 1 .9 1

Numerator:
= 238

Denominator:
= 5.1

CoG:
= 238 / 5.1
= **46.67**

Final answer.

---

## Geometrical CoG

If graph split into triangles/rectangles:

x* = Σ(Ai xi) / ΣAi

Ai = area
xi = centroid

Weighted average of areas.

---

# Center of Sum (CoS)

Formula:

x* = Σ(Ai xi) / ΣAi

Looks similar to geometric CoG.

Difference:
In **CoS**, overlapping area is counted **multiple times**.

In **CoG**, overlap counted **once**.

Important exam point.

---

# Center of Largest Area (CoA)

If multiple regions exist:
Choose region with **largest area** only.

Then use its center.

Simple.
