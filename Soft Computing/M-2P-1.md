# 8. Genetic Representation (Encoding Techniques)

Before solving any problem using GA, I first decide **how to represent my solution**.

This is called **encoding / representation**.

Bad representation = bad GA performance.

---

## 8.1 Binary Representation

Most common and simplest.

Uses only **0 and 1**.

Example:

`101101`

Used when problem has **yes/no** decisions.

Example:
0/1 Knapsack

- 1 = selected
- 0 = not selected

Example:

`10110`

Meaning:
pick item 1, 3, 4.

---

## 8.2 Permutation Representation

Used when **order matters**.

Example:
Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP)

Route:

`[1,3,2,4]`

Means:
visit city 1 → 3 → 2 → 4

Used in:
- routing
- scheduling
- sequencing

---

## 8.3 Real-Valued Representation

Uses decimal values directly.

Example:

`[0.5, 0.2, 0.8, 0.7]`

Useful in:
- continuous optimization
- engineering problems

Example:
temperature, speed, weight

---

## 8.4 Integer Representation

Uses integers.

Example:

`[1,2,3,4]`

Used for:
- IDs
- room numbers
- machine assignments

---

# 9. Important GA Terminology

## Individual
One possible solution.

Example:

`10101`

---

## Population
Group of individuals.

Example:

`10101`
`11100`
`00011`

---

## Search Space
All possible solutions.

Example for 3 bits:

`000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111`

---

## Chromosome
Complete encoded solution.

Example:

`101011`

---

## Gene
Single unit of chromosome.

Example:

`1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1`

---

## Allele
Value of gene.

Example:
0 or 1

---

## Genome
Complete set of chromosomes of an individual.

---

# 10. Genotype and Phenotype

## Genotype
Encoded solution.

Example:

`101101`

Computer understands this.

---

## Phenotype
Actual meaning after decoding.

Example:

`101101 → x = 45`

Human understands this.

---

## Encoding
Real solution → coded solution

Example:

`25 → 11001`

---

## Decoding
Coded solution → real solution

Example:

`11001 → 25`

---

# 11. Fitness Function

Fitness tells:

**How good is the solution?**

Higher fitness = better solution.

Example:

f(x) = x²

If x = 5

fitness = 25

---

# 12. Selection in GA

Goal:
choose parents for reproduction.

---

## 12.1 Roulette Wheel Selection (Fitness Proportionate)

Probability depends on fitness.

Formula:

P(i) = fᵢ / Σf

Meaning:
higher fitness → higher chance.

Steps:
1. Calculate total fitness
2. Generate random number
3. Select based on cumulative fitness

---

## 12.2 Tournament Selection

Pick **K random chromosomes**.

Best among them becomes parent.

Example:

A=5, B=8, C=3

Parent = B

Simple and fast.

---

## 12.3 Rank Selection

Used when fitness values are very close.

Example:

8.10, 8.05, 8.00, 7.99

Instead of fitness,
assign ranks.

Higher rank = better chance.

Useful near convergence.

---

# 13. Reproduction

Creating children from parents.

Uses:

1. Crossover
2. Mutation

---

# 14. Crossover

Combines genes of parents.

Purpose:
create better offspring.

---

## 14.1 One-Point Crossover

Choose one cut.

Example:

Parent1: `111|000`
Parent2: `000|111`

Child: `111111`

---

## 14.2 Two-Point / Multi-Point Crossover

Choose two cuts.

Swap middle part.

Example:

`11|100|0`
`00|011|1`

Result:
`11|011|0`

---

## 14.3 Uniform Crossover

No fixed cuts.

Treat every gene independently.

Like flipping a coin.

Example:

Parent1: `111000`
Parent2: `000111`

Possible child:

`101010`

More randomness.

---

# 15. Mutation

Small random change in chromosome.

Purpose:
- maintain diversity
- avoid local optimum
- prevent premature convergence

Mutation probability:
low (Pm)

Too high → random search

---

## 15.1 Bit Flip Mutation

Used in binary GA.

Example:

Before:
`001101`

After:
`001001`

Bit flipped.

---

## 15.2 Random Resetting

Used in integer representation.

Example:

`[2,4,6,8]`
→
`[2,7,6,8]`

One value reset.

---

## 15.3 Swap Mutation

Swap two positions.

Example:

`123456`
→
`126453`

Common in permutation.

---

## 15.4 Scramble Mutation

Choose subset and shuffle.

Example:

`123456789`
choose `3456`

After:
`123645789`

---

## 15.5 Inversion Mutation

Choose subset and reverse.

Example:

`123456789`
choose `3456`

After:
`126543789`

Difference:
reverse, not shuffle.

---

# 16. Population Initialization

How first generation starts.

---

## Random Initialization

Generate random solutions.

Example:

`10101`
`01011`

---

## Heuristic Initialization

Use domain knowledge.

Example:
nearest-neighbor in TSP

Better starting point.

---

# 17. Population Rules

## Diversity
Population should not be too similar.

Otherwise:
premature convergence.

---

## Population Size
Too large → slow  
Too small → weak solutions

Choose balanced size.

---

# 18. Termination Conditions

When should GA stop?

---

## Condition 1: No Improvement

If best solution not improving for y generations.

Stop.

---

## Condition 2: Max Generations

Example:
100 generations complete.

Stop.

---

## Condition 3: Desired Fitness Achieved

Target reached.

Stop immediately.

Best case.

---

# 19. Full GA Workflow

1. Initialize population
2. Evaluate fitness
3. Selection
4. Crossover
5. Mutation
6. New population
7. Check termination
8. Return best solution

Repeat until done.
